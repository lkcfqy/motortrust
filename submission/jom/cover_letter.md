Dear Editor,

I submit the research article entitled *Frozen Cross-Dataset Reliability of
Healthy-Only PMSM Stator-Fault Detection* for consideration in the *Journal of Magnetics*.

The article examines whether stator-fault alarms fitted and calibrated on healthy
currents retain reliability across datasets. It evaluates current asymmetry and
harmonic features under changing operating and measurement conditions, using a
frozen pipeline and target-only controls.

The exploratory KAIST result was 95.71% fault-block detection with 0/42 healthy
alarms. After a pre-reveal internal Git freeze, the primary Log-Euclidean detector
detected 96/384 external fault blocks (25.00%), produced 1/32 healthy alarms and
attained AUROC 0.6354. Its descriptive two-sided 95% Wilson upper endpoint of 15.74% exceeded the
prespecified 12% health gate. The preimplemented target-only MinCovDet comparator
reached 70.05% detection with 0/32 healthy alarms at the primary seed, but its gate
passed for only three of five seeds. The failed primary result is retained.

All 48 external fault records came from one physical PMSM. The article reports
turn–phase confounding, unequal pooled-AUROC load support, time dependence and
absence of real-time evidence. The internal Git freeze was not an externally
registered preregistration. A secondary parser failure is retained; its repair is
post-reveal and non-confirmatory. These findings connect electromagnetic diagnostics
with a concrete engineering failure: high same-family detection and late record-any
alarms did not demonstrate reliable transfer.

The supplement preserves all comparators, seed variation and bounded diagnostics.
The code package includes protocols, fine-grain results and data-acquisition commands.
Source-augmentation contrasts describe complete configurations, including different
fit-derived feature masks.
